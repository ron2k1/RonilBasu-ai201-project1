Hi Professor, I'd just like to say as a student I really appreciate you going the extra mile to make our lives easier. We appreciate professors like you very much.

Here's a list of suggestions I can think of off the top of my head:

​

- Please, please, please record your lectures and upload them somewhere they won't expire (youtube, etc). I understand that students should be accountable for attendance but I found it extremely helpful when I wanted to go back over a topic covered in lecture and I could just go back and rewatch the lesson.

- I've been hearing that some friends of mine aren't being added to course websites for summer classes they registered for in a timely manner. There's a good chance a few students might miss the first lecture or two, so being caught up would be a great help (this goes hand in hand with my first point). It's going to be syllabus week but you still don't want to start off behind.

- Odds are there's probably a video or outline somewhere on the internet on the topics covered which could prove more effective than a lecture for some students. Though imo it's mostly on the student to take the initiative to reach out for help and find them on their own if they're confused, having additional resources provided could only help.

- Online classes are more "isolated" if that makes sense. You're not actually together with the class, and in an inherently social class like yours it could be a bit awkward at first to build a sense of community and perform group tasks. My calc professor did a good job of dealing with this by spending the first five minutes or so sharing some random things he recently found interesting to make the class more comfortable - he called them "humanizing activities". Later on it felt more natural to ask questions and the like

- The online class that gave me the most grief kept shifting around due dates and grade weights last minute. Please don't.

That's about it, best of luck!

--- reply 5 (12 votes) ---
I am SO glad you asked—thank you.

Office Hours

Please try and set-up at least one hour a week where you jump on Zoom and have an open forum. So many profs teaching online course just put it on auto-pilot and think that is enough but without regular check-ins, many students slip behind or just feel too disengaged with the material (both, me).

An important aspect of learning is exchanging ideas and the last thing a student needs it to have more conversations in their own head. Listening and reacting to others is so important.

This semester, if my professor won't do it, I am going to try and arrange one among students. I hate the isolation of Canvas assignments. I mean, if I wanted to teach myself, I would have done so already.

Canvas Calendar

So many of my professors set stuff up in Canvas, but to not take the time to plug the due dates into the calendar—which we can subscribe to. At the beginning of every semester, I usually have to spend a whole day plugging in deadlines, and I sometimes enter them in wrong. And, if you are one of the ones who do (bless you), make sure to enter midnight deadlines as 11:59, or the alerts may show correctly.

As far as ideas for engagement…

Zoom is going to be great for this class as you have a ready-platform to broadcast from. When I have to do a webinar, I usually stand for the first part of the presentation to remind myself that it is a more normal event than a meeting.

I also think that tech glitches are par for the course—they happen in-person as well as online. In fact, I might stage some to teach students how to work-through and recover from them.

Also, I would mix up the teaching style. Recorded presentations are great but dialogue is always better than a one-way conversation. I might schedule some weeks live-virtually with the class so the modules have some variety. (You could also record these in case a student cannot make the live event.)

Hi! It’s nice to see professors looking for genuine feedback from students.

What I found worked well was using voice recordings over each individual PowerPoint slide. It really helped me digest the info being presented, I was able to pause/rewind for each snippet of info & I could review this info at a time that was convenient for me.

I had a professor who maintained a 3 hour Monday night lecture where he just presented his own slide deck and it was torture and I can confidently say I didn’t retain anything from that class.

I also found the weekly Q&A sessions helpful - made the professors connect with us more and it was a lot more personal than even in person lectures were because the professors shared their complaints as well!

Good luck!
