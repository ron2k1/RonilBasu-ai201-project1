THREAD: Major instructional cuts coming

New Brunswick departments are being told to cut a combined 25% from their Department Operational Budget and their Lecturer Budget. The operational budget covers non-salary stuff like seminars, printers, and events, while the lecturer budget covers the salaries at Rutgers. "Lecturer" is the Rutgers term for an adjunct—that is, part-time—instructor. University-wide, 1,300 Lecturers teach at least 30% of all undergraduate courses and hundreds more work in the professional and graduate schools.

In my moderate-sized department, this will mean cutting 45% of our Lecturer positions, and cutting almost 25% of our undergraduate sections. That, in turn, means that our class sizes will increase by about 30%.

Departments were told on Monday that they had until today to decide how they wanted to make the cuts; otherwise, the deans will make the decision. This is after two years of budget cuts in the School of Arts & Sciences, the biggest school at Rutgers-NB.

Why is this happening? No one really knows. There's no financial crisis. Word is the directive comes directly from President Tate, but he hasn't said anything. Chancellor Conway (responsible for Rutgers-NB) sent out a letter on Friday calling for a "stand-still expense budget." Since much of Rutgers' expenses are contractually obligated, in practice this means a cut-back budget for discretionary items, and Lecturer salaries are the biggest piece of that.

Enrollment is up– we should be offering more classes, not fewer.

President Tate came to Rutgers from LSU, and brought along lots of administrators from there. It feels like he's trying to make Rutgers more like LSU: good sports, lousy academics.
