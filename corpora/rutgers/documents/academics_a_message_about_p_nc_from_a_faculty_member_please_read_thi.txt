A faculty member has commented on the P/NC megathread about converting grades for this semester, future semester and ramifications of doing so on your future. I have linked the comment above as well as included the text below. Read it especially if you are considering a masters degree. I asked them to make a post, but since they used a throwaway they’re probably not checking comment replies

Hey faculty member (who isn't Dr. G) that lurks on here. I also do a fair bit of advising for students who are planning on going to graduate programs. There has been a lot of conversation among admissions people about what to do with P/NC in terms of applying for graduate admissions. I wanted to share the gist of what I am hearing from people involved with admissions.

Everyone is taking last spring with a grain of salt. Everything went crazy, they get it. This semester they are looking at people having P/NC on their transcripts a bit more skeptically. What they are saying is that if you have a reason for P/NC (got sick, caring for family, etc) then you should include an addendum (if allowed) to your application explaining why (for this Fall) you took P/NC. If you have a reason why P/NC then there is less skepticism, according to them. However, if you are going P/NC without a good reason (good in their eyes) then there is some pause when they see that. Additionally, there are people who are using P/NC to maintain 4.0's. If they see all A's and then a few P/NC's they know you didn't get an A and will not view your GPA as a 4.0 (sorry). Again, this is speaking about this Fall and NOT last spring.

Basically, from what I have heard from people involved in graduate admissions, is use P/NC sparingly (if you plan on going to graduate school of some form) and if you do have a reason why you're doing so. I couldn't be bothered to stay on top of my materials, got distracted, or didn't like online education isn't going to be viewed as a valid excuse for having multiple P/NC's on your transcripts this semester. So before you go and change several (or apparently 8) of your classes to P/NC think about what your goals are after college and evaluate what's best for your situation.

TLDR: don’t go wild with converting classes to a P this semester if you plan on doing a masters degree. The same can be said for jobs that ask for transcripts.
